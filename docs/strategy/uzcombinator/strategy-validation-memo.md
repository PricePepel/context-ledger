## 1. Verdict

**This is fundable as a regional seed business. It becomes venture-scale only if the enterprise that receives AI-built code is the one paying, with agencies as the wedge.**

| Outcome | Estimate | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 10 paying customers in 90 days | **~30%** | The product works. About 35 agencies in Uzbekistan fit today (model, UNVERIFIED). There have been no interviews or paid pilots yet. |
| UzCombinator Batch 3 acceptance | **~4% as-is; ~12–15%** with a co-founder, 20+ interviews and 2–3 LOIs | Base rate 2.0% (B1), 0.73% (B2) ([FAQ](https://uzcombinator.uz/en/faq)) |

**For**
- Investors are funding provenance: Entire raised a $60M seed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/10/former-github-ceo-raises-record-60m-dev-tool-seed-round-at-300m-valuation/)), and agent-governance startups are raising seeds of $4–10M ([pulse.bot](https://www.pulse.bot/entrepreneurship/news/general-analysis-snares-10m-in-seed-funding-cf600633-fd84-4c8b-94aa-ef16ffa4fd84/)).
- The pain is current: 96% of developers don't fully trust AI code, and only 48% always check it ([Sonar](https://www.sonarsource.com/company/press-releases/sonar-data-reveals-critical-verification-gap-in-ai-coding/)). 54% of agency clients hold the agency responsible for AI outcomes ([AMI](https://agencymanagementinstitute.com/podcasts/2026-agency-edge-insights/)).
- Yurideks, the closest alumnus, raised $250K without revenue ([profile](https://uzcombinator.uz/en/startup-directory/yurideks)).

**Against**
- Applications close **10 Nov 2026, 23:59**, four days from now. The site also shows a conflicting "34 days left" counter ([home](https://uzcombinator.uz/en)).
- Dev tools and compliance are not in the Batch 3 RFS ([RFS](https://uzcombinator.uz/en/rfs)), and the FAQ calls solo founders "harder" ([FAQ](https://uzcombinator.uz/en/faq)).
- There is no Uzbek evidence that clients demand provenance. Only 7.2% of the population uses AI regularly ([Kun.uz](https://kun.uz/en/news/2026/06/01/kazakhstan-leads-central-asia-in-ai-adoption-as-uzbekistan-struggles-with-low-usage-rates-0fddc4)).
- Entire could ship a handover export in 1–3 months ([GitHub](https://github.com/entireio/cli)).

## 2. Positioning for UzCombinator

**One-liner:** "Ledger Handover turns every AI coding-agent session into a signed, human-approved receipt, so enterprises can accept AI-built software from outside vendors with proof of who decided what, and why."

**Venture framing: signed receipts for AI-built software.** A cross-vendor attestation layer in the style of in-toto/SLSA. Agencies distribute it and enterprise procurement pays to verify. Later it expands into trust badges, insurance and audit integrations.

**RFS answer:** "Local AI governance infrastructure for Uzbekistan's IT export sector," tied to the national target of $5B in IT exports by 2030 ([Kun.uz](https://kun.uz/en/news/2025/12/27/uzbekistan-aims-to-raise-it-services-exports-to-5bn-by-2030)).

**Why an Uzbek team wins the beachhead**
- 1,050 IT Park residents export, and 54% of exports go to North America ([IT Park](https://www.it-park.uz/en/itpark/news/it-park-members-export-geography-reaches-102-countries)). Exports were $728.4M in 2025 ([siat.stat.uz](https://siat.stat.uz/data/3358/?lang=en)).
- Export quotas rise from 20% in 2026 to 50% in 2028, so keeping foreign clients becomes mandatory ([intellinews](https://www.intellinews.com/uzbekistan-mandates-export-targets-for-it-park-residents-327704)).
- The team can reach agencies through IT Park and UzCombinator's 51 partners ([partners](https://uzcombinator.uz/en/partners)), access a US forge cannot buy.
- Costs are low: 0% corporate tax until 2028 and 7.5% income tax on wages ([IT Park](https://www.it-park.uz/index.php/en/itpark/news/tax-incentives-for-startups-and-foreign-investors-in-uzbekistan-s-it-sector)).
- Prove it in Uzbekistan, then expand to Kazakhstan, the Caucasus and Eastern Europe.

## 4. Customer

**ICP:** an IT Park exporter with 3–30 staff doing fixed-price work for US or EU enterprise or regulated clients. It already runs Claude Code or Codex and has a founder or PM who signs off deliverables. Buying triggers are milestone acceptance, contract end, or an AI-disclosure clause in the contract ([Mike Young Law](https://mikeyounglaw.com/should-your-software-development-agreement-disclose-ai-written-code/)).

**Top 15 targets.** All come from the [Clutch Uzbekistan list](https://clutch.co/uz/developers) and are ordered by signs of regulated clients. Enterprise and fixed-price fit is UNVERIFIED for each.
1. UzVIP, has a reviewed fintech project ([Clutch](https://clutch.co/go-to-review/uzvip/279116))
2. USOFT Round Umbrella, serves banks and fintech ([Clutch](https://clutch.co/profile/umbrella-soft))
3. ZK-Digital (50–249 staff, so a test of larger accounts)

Ranks 4–15: Udevs, Exadot, IT Brick, Tezcode, SSD Software Solutions, Azzimut, IOTA.UZ, ShahNur Software, Appx Group (Urganch), Automatic Technology Solutions (Qarshi), NAPA Team, Digerati Group.

Backup: w2w ([GoodFirms](https://www.goodfirms.co/company/w2w)). The research found no non-Uzbek targets, so build that list in month 2.

**Pain evidence, ranked**
1. **Disclosure (most monetizable).** 65% of clients call disclosure of how their data is protected "essentially required" ([AMI](https://agencymanagementinstitute.com/podcasts/2026-agency-edge-insights/)). A GSA draft rule would require federal contractors to disclose AI systems within 30 days of award ([JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/gsa-ai-procurement-rules-would-6012707/)). Only 50% of agencies have discussed AI use with clients ([Business of Story](https://businessofstory.com/ad-agency-ai-expertise/)).
2. **AI-trust (hardest numbers).** Sonar's 96% vs 48% gap above. Only 24% of organizations check AI code for IP and security ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/less-than-a-quarter-of-organizations-are-securing-ai-generated-code)).
3. **Context loss (loud, but free tools exist).** See [claude-code #43696](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/43696) and one agency's published Claude Code handover checklist ([eSEOspace](https://eseospace.com/blog/claude-code-handover-documentation/)).
4. **Handover (long-standing).** The reasons behind decisions are harder to recover than the code ([Thoughtworks](https://thoughtworks.com/insights/blog/lessons-inheriting-another-team-s-codebase)).

The weakest signal is acceptance disputes: no case law was found.

|---|---|---|---|

| Git AI, Span | Line-level provenance, AI-code detection | Free / enterprise | No decisions; Git AI could be an import source |
| Claude Code / Codex native | Local JSONL files; Claude Code deletes them after 30 days by default | Bundled | Single vendor, unsigned |

**Wedge:** the deliverable at the moment code changes hands. The client can verify it without trusting the agency. It works on client-owned GitHub, GitLab or Azure repos and is billed as a project line item. The window is about 6–12 months ([Entire](https://entire.com/news/entire-launches-an-independent-git-forge-for-agent-human-collaboration)).

**Three must-have features**
1. Every claim cites its source message, and a named reviewer must approve it before export.
2. An Ed25519-signed packet that can be verified offline, with a redaction attestation manifest and a free verifier that needs no account.
3. SOW-mapped delta handover: claims are mapped to contract deliverables, with a diff against the last signed packet.

## 8. Product requirements the research changes

**Add as P0**
- Free offline verifier (CLI and static web page)
- Ed25519 packet signing, with the reviewer's identity in the signature
- Approve or reject each claim, with export blocked until every claim is resolved
- Redaction attestation manifest, with a synthetic red-team suite that must pass before release
- SOW mapping and delta packets
- An "AI-use disclosure" contract attachment generated from each packet
- Importers for Entire checkpoints, Git AI notes and SpecStory, so competitors become inputs

**Reprioritize or cut**
- Keep memory, search and MCP in the open-source core but stop leading with them. Memory is free, and Entire is funded to own it.
- Graph validation becomes internal plumbing for linking claims.
- Drop per-seat pricing.
- Drop EU AI Act urgency from the pitch. High-risk obligations are slipping to Dec 2027 ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines/)).
- Keep Russia out of the base plan.

**Later:** a dashboard for recipients, and SOC 2 / ISO 27001 evidence mapping once auditors have validated it.

## 9. Top risks: 30-day de-risking

| Risk | 30-day test | Pivot signal |
|---|---|---|
| Agencies see client-facing proof as a liability | 20 interviews among exporters; ask for a $125 pre-payment | Fewer than 3 of 20 would show a packet to a client |
| Clients don't ask for it | 3 agencies each send a sample packet to one client | No recipient verifies it or asks for more |
| Low agent usage in Uzbekistan | Screening question in every interview | Under 50% use agents: lead with Kazakhstan and Eastern Europe |
| Entire ships an export | Ship the verifier, the Entire importer and an open packet schema | Entire adds signed approvals: reposition as client-side verification |
| Fixed-price share and handover hours unknown | Ask how many hours the last handover took; pull Clutch size bands | Under 8 hours: price per milestone |
| Solo founder hurts acceptance | Recruit a co-founder before 10 Nov; apply to Startup Garage and IT Park Ventures in parallel | If rejected: reapply next batch with traction |
| A redaction leak destroys trust | Red-team set in CI with a leak rate of 0 | Any leak blocks export |
